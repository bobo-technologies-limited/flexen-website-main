Importing from China to Kenya: A Practical Guide

Understand the major stages involved in sourcing, purchasing, shipping and receiving goods from China into Kenya.

China is an important sourcing market for businesses looking for a wide range of products and manufacturing opportunities. For Kenyan businesses, successful importing requires careful planning from supplier selection through to shipping and clearance.

1. Decide What You Want to Source

Start with a clear product requirement. Define the product specifications, quantity, quality expectations, packaging and intended use.

2. Find Potential Suppliers

Identify suppliers whose products and capabilities match your requirements. Comparing several supplier options can help you understand the available commercial choices.

3. Request and Compare Quotations

Ask suppliers for detailed quotations. Review product specifications, quantities, packaging, lead times, minimum order quantities and payment terms alongside the quoted price.

4. Negotiate the Commercial Terms

Once suitable suppliers have been identified, discuss pricing and other commercial terms before confirming the procurement.

5. Confirm the Order

Before payment, ensure the agreed product specifications, quantities, packaging and commercial terms are clearly understood.

6. Arrange Payment

Proceed with payment according to the agreed procurement arrangement and supplier terms.

7. Arrange Shipping from China

Shipping arrangements should take into account the cargo, volume, destination, preferred transport method and delivery requirements.

8. Prepare for Arrival in Kenya

Before the goods arrive, businesses should ensure that the required import documentation and applicable clearance arrangements are being addressed.

9. Clearance and Delivery

Once the goods arrive, they may need to go through the applicable customs and clearance procedures before being released for onward delivery.

10. Calculate Your Total Landed Cost

When evaluating whether an import is commercially viable, consider more than the supplier's quoted unit price.

The overall cost may include procurement, packaging, transportation, applicable duties and taxes, clearance, handling and delivery expenses.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Businesses can reduce sourcing problems by defining their requirements clearly, comparing suppliers, understanding the full procurement cost and planning logistics before placing an order.
