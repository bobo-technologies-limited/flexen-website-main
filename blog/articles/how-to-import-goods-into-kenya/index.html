How to Import Goods into Kenya

A practical overview of the major stages involved in sourcing, shipping, clearing and receiving imported goods in Kenya.

Importing goods involves several stages, from deciding what to purchase and identifying a supplier to arranging transportation, completing clearance requirements and receiving the goods.

Step 1: Define What You Want to Import

Start by clearly defining the product, quantity, specifications, intended use and destination. Clear requirements make supplier research and cost comparison easier.

Step 2: Identify a Suitable Supplier

Research potential suppliers and compare their products, specifications, pricing, minimum order quantities and commercial terms.

Step 3: Confirm the Product Requirements

Confirm product specifications before placing an order. Depending on the product, businesses may also need to consider applicable standards, permits or regulatory requirements.

Step 4: Agree on Commercial Terms

Before procurement, clarify the supplier quotation, quantities, packaging, payment arrangements and expected production or preparation timeline.

Step 5: Arrange Payment

Once the supplier and commercial terms have been approved, arrange payment using the agreed procurement process.

Step 6: Arrange Shipping

Determine the appropriate shipping method based on the cargo, quantity, destination and required delivery timeline.

Step 7: Prepare for Clearance

Imported goods may require documentation and clearance procedures before they can be released for delivery. Importers should understand the applicable requirements for their particular goods.

Step 8: Account for Import Costs

Businesses should consider the full procurement cost, including the supplier price, transportation, applicable duties, taxes, clearance costs and other charges.

Step 9: Clear and Receive the Goods

Once the necessary procedures have been completed, the goods can proceed through the appropriate delivery arrangements.

Why Planning Matters

International procurement becomes easier when sourcing, payment, shipping and clearance are considered as part of one process rather than separate activities.
